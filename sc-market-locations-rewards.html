Introduction

Get ready for a BIG one today. It's a full strategy guide for the Market Deck, Locations, and Rewards Pile from Star Trek Captain's Chair: Second Contact! When using these cards, you will mix them in their respective market decks along with at least one standalone box (right now, the core set or To Boldly Go). The market will thus be slightly expanded. You will ALWAYS use these cards when playing with Captains Pike, Riker, and Freeman. There's five Person cards, three Cargo Cards, two Ship Cards, and three Ally Cards. Additionally, we get two new locations and an eight Person Rewards Pile. Can't wait any longer? Let's dive in.

Persons

D'Erika Tendi (Person)

Notable Appearances: LDS 4x4: Something Borrowed, Something Green, 4x10: Old Friends, New Planets, 5x1: Dos Cerritos, & 5x2: Shades of Green

D'Erika Tendi is the current leader of House Tendi of the Orion Syndicate as well as D'Vana Tendi's younger sister. She becomes the head of her family when D'Vana leaves for Starfleet. It was cool to see Lower Decks build on the Matriarchal Orion control that was only briefly touched on before. And while the Syndicate is discussed in detail during DS9, there aren't actually a lot (if any?) Orions seen during DS9. It's interesting to add that background to previous years of scant information.

D'Erika has an attack play action which requires you to discard a card to choose two of the following four: Steal a Latinum, gain a Ship from the Junk, gain an Influence, or gain a Military. If you have another Orion in play you may exhaust a ship to do up to all four. Her Attack Activation requires you to spend two Latinum to send an Away Team to a neutral location, and you may remove an opponent Away Team from the same Location to gain a glory. Often when removing opponent Away teams, you'll need a ship or Away Team already at the location you're removing your opponent from, so this is nice that you send the Away Team first, then remove an opponent's. I think even just gaining a Ship from the Junk makes this a desirable card, but the rest of the text is great too, especially if you've got a few Orion locations out or market cards in your deck. Between this, her sister in Freeman's deck, and the new Location, the Orion Syndicate synergy is really coming into its own.

Jennifer Sh'reyan (Person)

Notable Appearances: LDS 2x6: The Spy Humongous, 3x6: Hear All, Trust Nothing, & 5x3: The Best Exotic Nanite Hotel

Member of the Cerritos crew, Jennifer and Mariner are initially enemies, then a couple, then not really anything except Jennifer thought they were still dating? It's confusing. Don't be in any rush to meet her friends. They call their hangouts salons. Like, what are you, Hemingway?

Jennifer features the new Lower Decker trait in addition to her Pilot, Starfleet, and Andorian ones. Her free-play action lets you beam her to a deployed ship to gain two Dilithium, and you may recall another card beamed to that ship and may warp that ship. Her Support ability triggers after you gain a Person (at which time you would play Jennifer from your hand), then you draw a card and you may send an Away Team to a controlled location. Her Activation as duty officer lets you warp a ship to draw two cards and discard one. This card doesn't do anything groundbreaking, but it's doing a bunch of little things with no action token costs, and that can really add up. Dilithium gain can be crucial for certain Captains without Utilize (Archer and Burnham) and she provides another way to get card draw and Away Teams out. And if you'll notice, her Activation is a version of Maximum Warp, and like so many Lower Deckers it reflects basic directives being performed by the crew of the Cerritos (See the upcoming Freeman Guide for more details there).

K'ranch (Person)

Notable Appearance: LDS 3x2: The Least Dangerous Game

A Kromsapiod, K'ranch is one of those species that loves to hunt (like an off-brand Hirogen) and he gets restless enough to request that Tendi and Bomler be his prey. Only Tendi is wise enough to say "no, thank you." When he logs a card, don't think of it as killing. Kromsapoids are all about "catch and release."

Kranch gets a military skill in addition to Alien (the first in the Person deck!) and Weapon traits. His play action gains you a military, draws a card, and then you may log that card for two glory and optionally promote K'ranch to Duty Officer. As Duty Officer, K'ranch lets you log cards you gain for a Dilithium and card draw, which is great for gaining points while keeping your deck lean. But most importantly, if you're looking to rush the endgame, this is a fantastic card to have in your deck. Two Glory will speed things up by one round, so its a great way to undercut an opponent who hasn't gotten their engine up and running.

Ma'ah (Person)

Notable Appearances: LDS 2x9: wej duj, 4x9: The Inner Fight, 5x4: A Farewell to Farms, & 5x10: The New Next Generation

Ma'ah gets quite a few appearances in Lower decks, working his way up to Captain then getting Nick Locarno'd to blood wine wholesaler. Whether he's standing up against a dishonorable Captain or falling into the mud with his Targ, he keeps trying to find upward mobility in the Empire. As a sidenote, I recently watched DS9 5x21: Soldiers of the Empire, where Jadzia Dax says that Klingons can only challenge their direct superior. When Ma'ah challenges Dorg, he had recently been promoted to first officer. It was excellent continuity.

Ma'ah is a Klingon Lower Decker, and his Support action triggers when another Klingon is put into play, gaining you a ship at the cost of an Incident, or an Action Token and discarding an Incident. I think that his support action is best used as an off-brand Analyze, having the same effect of gaining a Ship at the cost of Taking and Incident, only without having to pay any action tokens. His play action dismisses your Duty Officer (someone's got to overthrow bad Klingon captains) to gain a Military, Influence, and Glory (for the Empire!). As Duty Officer, he can log a Klingon/Shady/Lower Decker from your hand or discard pile to gain an Influence and draw two cards, which is extremely flexible, especially in Captain Freeman or Koloth's decks. I really like Ma'ah as an addition to the game, though four out of five of these new person cards have random apostrophes which can be hard to type!

Parmen (Person)

Notable Episode: TOS 3x12: Plato's Stepchildren

The leader of the Platonians, Parmen was a member of an Ancient (thus the trait) humanoid species that based their civilization on the Greeks, who they met when they briefly resettled on Earth. He also messed with Kirk and the Enterprise crew because he wanted McCoy to stay with them as their resident (alien) doctor.

Parmen has two free-play actions. The first is an attack that lets you find any card in your draw deck, and exhaust an opponent's duty officer and Captain. This is incredible, and it should be noted that even if you can't exhaust both the captain and duty officer (because there is no DO or they're already exhausted) you can still find the card because the second sentence isn't a cost of performing the first. Using Parmen to find a card in your Draw deck, especially for free, will lead to some great control over your turns. The second Play action gives you an influence and Latinum for a card discard. As Duty Officer, Parmen can free play gained Person cards so long as you meet the six influence requirement. I like everything I'm seeing here, and would gladly add Parmen to my crew (probably because he's telepathically forcing me to do so).

Cargo

Gift Box (Cargo)

Notable Appearances: TNG 1x11: Haven & LDS 4x3: In the Cradle of Vexilon

First off, can I just say that it's always nice getting more traits for aliens we don't see as often? We've got two Betazoid trait cards in this expansion, and the unquestionably bizarre gift box is a perfect use for it. The gift box was first shown being given to Counselor Troi as a wedding gift, but we've also seen it a couple times on Lower Decks, most notably in conjunction with the Wadi Chula game ("Allamaraine!" "Did I win?" "Hardly!").

The Gift Box deploys, while letting you discard a card to gain a cargo, which is fitting since this is cargo that carries cargo. Its Activation lets you beam a Cargo from your Discard pile while your opponent draws a card, and the Resupply lets you recall a card beamed to the Gift Box. The Reaction gains you one Latinum when you put a Betazoid (including this card) in play. Beaming cards to Persons (Landru in To Boldly Go) and Cargo feels thematically a bit off compared to beaming to ships or locations, but I think it would have been weird and confusing to have to come up with another thematic term for the same action. I like the Gift Box, especially because you can use it to keep your cargo from entering your deck, thus making it available after repeated actions while keeping the deck thin.

Moopsy (Cargo)

Notable Appearance: LDS 4x2: I Have No Bones Yet I Must Flee

"Holy F--k, the Moopsy is free!" The Moopsy drinks your bones! Story time: My kids saw me watching Lower Decks on my phone when this episode came out and asked me what this creature was, and my oldest literally asked me every night for six months if the Moopsy was going to come and eat his bones. Bad parenting moment, excellent addition to Star Trek Captain's Chair.

Moopsy's play action deploys for a Latinum and forces your opponent to log a Person card. Then, its Reaction lets you draw a card when you or your opponent logs a person. And on Resupply, you can refresh Moopsy and log a person (also triggering its reaction) and refresh it again. It's thirsty for those bones! In all seriousness, anybody who can reliably gain from a Synthetic-logging strategy should adore this card. The problem is you need a lot of Person cards to feed it, so be sure to have a steady stream of bones ready to go.

Protocol 12 (Cargo)

Notable Appearances: SNW 2x1: The Broken Circle & 2x8: Under the Cloak of War

A performance enhancing drug developed by Joseph M'Benga, Protocol 12 has appeared a couple times in Strange New Worlds, and suggests a darker side to the Enterprise's Chief Medical Officer.

With the Starfleet, Doctor, and Shady traits, Protocol 12 has an action that requires five Military, and for each other Doctor in play (max three) you can send an away team to a location, gaining a Glory if you send all three. If you've got a lot of Doctor cards, this is impressive. There's also a free play action where you gain a military at the cost of taking an Incident and logging a person or Directive in hand or in play. This is less impressive, but can be helpful for logging cards if you're struggling to find options for thinning your deck. There's also special text that gives your Doctor Person cards the Augment trait while Protocol 12 is in play, which could lead to some interesting combos with Khan or the Illyrians, but won't give you any benefit at Cold Station 12 as it will be out of play during Endgame scoring.

Ships

Fesarius (Ship)

Notable Appearance: TOS 1x2: The Corbomite Maneuver

The giant sphere piloted by baby Clint Howard, this ship gets played and warped as a free action. It flips the normal Activation costs, and can warp for free but costs a Dilithium to beam aboard. It also has another activation that requires you to log a Person with Starfleet beamed to Fesarius, discard a directive, and exhaust another ship to take the top encounter to your discard pile, then log Fesarius. It's another card that can get you an encounter outside of the normal Strange New Worlds paradigm, and it's always fun to see these pop up, especially with epic and classic ships like Fesarius.

R.I.S. Talvath (Ship)

Notable Appearance: VOY 1x7: Eye of the Needle

In an early episode of Voyager, they encountered a thirty-one centimeter wormhole that led to Romulan space and the R.I.S. Talvath, a Romulan science vessel. Unfortunately, it was also twenty years in the past, so even though they figured out how to beam material and people through the wormhole, they couldn't use it to return home without breaking temporal protocols.

The Talvath gets a deploy action along with a free warp, a Reaction that lets you put one Dilithium on the Talvath and warp it when you or your opponent puts an Anomaly, Communication or Time Travel in play, and a Passive that gains you a glory and all resources when the Talvath is dismissed during the Control step (watch out for cards that dismiss your ship outside the Control step). I love this ship thematically and I like the somewhat unique nature of its table operations.

Allies

Betazed Intelligence (Ally)

Notable Appearance: LDS 4x5: Empathological Fallacies

It's Romulan Ale O'clock somewhere, and the ladies of the Betazed Intelligence are ready to party. There's a good reference to Zanthi fever, something previously referenced in relation to Lwaxana. And the episode also has some Romulans lurking the Neutral Zone for good measure.

They have a free play action that lets you find a Beverage, Ambassador, or Telepath (except from your Reserve), which is all fine and good. But the kicker here is the Clean-up operation, which increases your hand size by one for each different Starfleet, Beverage, and Ambassador card you have in play. This is one of each, so your limit here would be three total, and you'd have to have all three of those traits in play (but you can have multiple traits on the same card, for instance, Enterprise C would count for the Starfleet and Ambassador). If you raise your hand size by two or three, you'll log the Betazeds, thus ending the party and requiring you to go back to work... playing this game.

Illyrians (Ally)

Notable Appearances: ENT 3x19: Damage & SNW 2x2: Ad Astra per Aspera

While Illyrians came up once before in Enterprise Season 3 (an episode in which Jean Luc Picard would find Starfleet's behavior appalling and Sisko could probably live with it, but you really have to watch the whole season for it to make sense), we've seen them discussed far more in Strange New Worlds. Una Chin Riley, or Pike's Number One, is revealed to be an Augmented Illyrian who faced discrimination due to Federation banning on genetic manipulation.

This Ally card has two play actions. The first is free, and allows you to promote a Person from hand or Staging Area to Duty Officer, which has plenty of obvious applications. The second requires you to discard a Starfleet to scan two of the Person deck, and promote the scanned card to Duty Officer. You can have up to two additional Duty Officers in play, which is nice because on Clean-up, you gain a Research for each Duty Officer in play. If you have no other Augment traits, Illyrians is logged, otherwise you discard it as normal from the staging area (reminds me of Halkan Council's log if Attacks are in play). I like the Duty Officer manipulation, but you're going to have to dismiss some of them at the end of the turn, so the benefits are limited. Still, thematic and interesting Ally.

Nova Fleet (Ally)

Notable Appearance: LDS 4x10: Old Friends, New Planets

A conglomeration of a bunch of Lower Deckers, Nova Fleet was formed (briefly) by Nick Locarno as the antagonist of season four of Lower Decks. It fell apart because of course, you can't have a leaderless coalition, especially made up of Ferengi and Romulan defectors.

Nova Fleet lets you gain an Ally or cargo from the junk (the leftovers of better captains) and then log Nova Fleet. Alternatively, when you junk a card from the market, you can gain it to your hand, taking an Incident if your opponent has a Lower Decker trait in play. This is much better, because not only can it be replicated multiple times, it gives you a free purchase and use of the card. I think this is a great way to encourage players to push into the Support operations of Lower Decker cards.

Locations

Orion (Advanced Location)

Notable Appearances: LDS 4x4: Something Borrowed, Something Green, 4x10: Old Friends, New Planets, 5x1: Dos Cerritos, & 5x2: Shades of Green

They're not all pirates... but there's a lot of pirates. Orion gets added to the Advanced Location deck with an Influence and Military skill. Its Control operation lets you gain a ship from the top of the deck and free play it, if able, for the low cost of Latinum. You can gain a Dilithium or Latinum and an Action token by logging a ship for its Activation, which also feels thematic for pirating. On endgame, you score a point for each Orion card (plus one printed VP), and since this expansion adds one that's something!

Krulmuth-B (Starting Location)

Notable Appearance: SNW 2x7: Those Old Scientists

Finally, you can add Krulmuth-B to the Starting location options. The Krulmuth-B portal has Nausicaan roots but was discovered by Captain Christopher Pike the Orion Science Vessel D'Var and uses horonium to activate time travel! But you don't care about any of that, what does the card do? Its Control operation lets you take an Incident to scan for an Orion, and its reaction lets you send an away team whenever you put a Scientist or Orion in play. But the Activation is most exciting, as you can remove an away team to look at two random Crossover cards, take one (and take means you'll get it right to your hand!) and destroy the other. That means in any given game you'll have four possible Crossover Person cards you can gain from Krulmuth-B!

Now, each standalone box comes with ten starting locations, so used with either box you will have four chances out of the eleven starting locations to have Krulmuth-B in your game. If you're combining all three boxes together, there's fifteen total starting locations, thus reducing your odds of seeing it at all. And, only one player is going to have a chance to get these special Crossover Person cards, though it may actually cost them points to do so. Most Crossover reward cards have a points penalty if they are not logged at the end of the game, which is fun because logging here thematically represents getting them back where they belong. No time travelers!.

Rewards Pile

Let's take a look at the Rewards pile:

Ambassador Spock (Person)

Notable Appearances: TNG 5x7-8: Unification

It's Spock from Unification, where he is working hard to reunite the Romulans and Vulcans despite his father Sarek's desired path for him. I like that another episode referenced in wave two, Journey to Babel, gave the origin of Spock's estrangement from his father. It's cool for Star Trek to have paid that off with a mind-meld with Picard over 20 years later. And Spock's mission to reunite with the Romulans has certainly shaped the post-TNG era Trek political landscape.

This version of Spock has Human, Vulcan, Telepath, Ambassador, and Crossover traits. His play action lets you log a card from your hand for three Dilithium and you may log a beamed card to scan for a card with a Research Focus. His reaction requires five Research, and you may gain on a track when you put an encounter in play. He also lets you have another Person with Vulcan, Romulan, or Ambassador on duty with Ambassador Spock at the same time. If the card isn't logged at the end of the game, it scores negative two points. I really enjoy this version of Spock, which is similar to both the Kirk deck Captain Spock and Pike deck Lt. Spock, but I like the small thematic differences for each.

Barry Waddle (Person)

Notable Appearance: DS9 5x6: Trials and Tribble-ations

Is that Arne Darvin? Barry Waddle appeared in and used the Bajoran Orb of Time to travel back to his greatest failure as a Klingon in deep cover.

I like the Business trait for him as that was his cover in the 24th century, and he has a familiar play action where he can steal two Dilithium, but may also spend two Latinum to scan for Influence focus. He still increases your hand size by one, but he also has a Reaction that requires five Influence and lets you refresh cards that share a trait with Darvin when you spend Latinum. He also scores negative two points if he's not logged at the end of the game. I love the similarities to Darvin from Koloth's deck, with a bit of a Latinum focused twist.

Chancellor Gowron (Person)

Notable Appearances: TNG 4x7: Reunion, DS9 4x1-2: The Way of the Warrior & 7x22: Tacking Into the Wind

Introduced in The Next Generation episode Reunion, Gowron appears a handful of times throughout TNG and DS9 as the head of the Klingon Empire. He and his googly-eyes get to see a Kahless clone, the rise of the Dominion, and Worf right before he dies.

He has the Klingon, Imperial, and Crossover traits, and has one Research and one Influence skill. His play action requires you to find a Klingon or a ship, and if it's both you gain a Glory. You may also spend three Dilithium to scan for a military focus card. His Reaction requires five military, triggers when you take control of a location, and you can choose two of these three: draw a card, junk a card, discard a card to gain a Glory. If not logged, Gowron scores negative two points. I like that his focus is on finding Klingon ships with a heavy military emphasis, and the Glory focus fits in nicely with the theme of Klingons and Glory, as well as the archetype of rushing the endgame.

Chef Riker (Person)

Notable Appearance: None, this never happened (fine, ENT 4x22: These are the Voyages)

When you first watch Enterprise, you'll notice they reference Chef a LOT in the first season. Too bad Chef is actually just Riker playing with holograms during the events of The Pegasus (TNG 7x12). Oh well, it's a Crossover that also suffers from being a series finale.

Chef Riker lets you draw a card from your discard pile for each different trait of Beverage, Engineer, and NX-01 you have in play. You can then duplicate a Person or Directive from your log, and you may promote Chef Riker. As Duty Officer, he has two familiar Activations. The first lets him free play a ship with Starfleet. The second lets you draw a Ship, Hologram, or NX-01 from your discard pile. If not logged, he scores you one negative point. I really like the idea of using this specifically with Archer, but the nice thing about NX-01 cards is that they're in the main person deck for anyone to swipe. Honestly, I kind of wish more captain-specific cards showed up in the main Person market. I love the familiarity between this Riker's Activations and the Riker in Picard's deck (and even the Riker captain).

Ensign Boimler (Person)

Notable Appearance: SNW 2x7: Those Old Scientists

Jack Quaid did not have an easy job. Voice acting for animation is a very different beast than having to translate that to live-action. He does so many good mannerisms of Boimler, from the weird power-walk to the Boimler scream. Whatever you may think of Strange New Worlds and Lower Decks (I personally am loving both), he has done an excellent job with this character.

Boimler's play action is free and lets you discard three cards to gain an Action Token, but he also has a support operation that lets you duplicate the play operation of a junked or promoted Person, which has such great flexibility. He can't be promoted (we definitely don't want this guy on the Enterprise bridge). If not logged, he scores negative two points. Boimler cards (there are 4 in this box!) have a lot of action token-related actions and duplicate support actions, so this feels perfect both mechanically and thematically.

Ensign Mariner (Person)

Notable Appearance: SNW 2x7: Those Old Scientists

Tawny Newsome did an equally good job as Jack Quaid (though she does have less physical humor elements of her character) and was quick on the quips during These Old Scientists. Hot Spock? Dystopian San Francisco. Their references are legendary, and oddly specific.

Ensign Mariner has a free play action that lets you gain a Latinum, and up to twice, take an Incident to your Discard pile to find any card, except in your Reserve. Her support lets you refresh a duty officer as it exhausts, and you can send an away team where you have a ship. She scores one negative point if you don't log her. I love that Mariner is high risk (take two Incidents!) for high rewards (find any card in your deck or discard?). Very on brand for a crazy yet competent Starfleet officer.

Lieutenant Dax (Person)

Notable Appearance: DS9 5x6: Trials and Tribble-ations

Another appearance from Trials and Tribble-ations (DS9 5x6), Lieutenant Dax is wearing the TOS era dress uniform for her covert mission to the Enterprise and Deep Space K-9. They intentionally keep her away from Koloth since he looks so different from the Koloth who was already on DS9, which would lead to some uncomfortable questions that won't be answered until Enterprise Season 4.

Lieutenant Dax has a research skill and familiar table operations. Her play action gains her one on any track, then you may spend a Latinum to gain on any track, and if you have a Vulcan duty officer you can gain on any track. You can then promote Dax to duty officer. Her Reaction lets you scan two and draw a card whenever you would gain a card from the market. If not logged, she scores one negative point. In addition to being a great card on its own, I like the flexibility it can give you to get up on the right tracks at the right time.

The Living Witness (Person)

Notable Appearance: VOY 4x23: The Living Witness

From the episode of the same name, this version of The Doctor is an unexplained backup EMG that is stolen during a raid by one of the many aggressive species they encounter during their trek home. However, hundreds of years later, he ends up in a museum where they've gotten the history really wrong, and he must set the record straight. This episode is a story within a story (as revealed at the end) and is probably the second furthest into the future we've ever gone in Star Trek, if not even further.

The Living Witness has Doctor, Starfleet, and Hologram traits. He has a free play action that lets you draw a card, free play an Incident, and if you have an Ancient in play you log The Living Witness and may find and return another Incident. Otherwise, he scores four points on Endgame, but he'll have to be in the Duty Officer position at the end of the game to score those points. No penalty if you can't pull it off (and since he's not incentivized to be logged, no logging penalty).
