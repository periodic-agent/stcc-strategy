Introduction
Another week, another Captain Guide from To Boldly Go! And I'm especially excited for this one because I finished watching Enterprise in October 2025, and so the thematic content is relatively fresh on my mind. Today's Captain is our first "Captain in spirit only": Ambassador Soval of the Vulcan. As such, it's our first (and maybe only) Vulcan captain deck, and it's focused on the era of the NX-01 Enterprise. As someone who didn't watch Enterprise as it was airing (I was a tween-teen, to be fair), I was shocked how much I enjoyed it, especially when it hit the later seasons. And Soval as the representative of a Vulcan that was far less an ally and far more an overcautious parent is strangely compelling. It was bumpy for Earth in those early years, and the Vulcans did what they could to slow Earth's warp development for fear of them becoming adversaries. Soval represents that well, reflecting a Vulcan society that is far more confused internally than it wants to admit. Also, if you would like to hear designer Adam Turczi's design notes for this deck, he posted them here (And I'd highly recommend reading this before reading my guide).

Basic Side
Advanced Side
Soval's player board's basic side track multipliers max out at 6x for all skill tracks. As always the advanced side's thresholds are one less. His basic mission is My Mind to Your Mind, which is kind of ironic considering how long it takes him to publicly embrace his ability to Mind Meld. He requires five cards with Research or Wild skills in play, not beamed, which is not only a good challenge but helps you with track advancement. The reward allows you, for each Telepath in play, to draw a card or gain a Latinum and you may Return an Incident. I have found myself using this for the card draw more often, especially if I have no Incidents in hand when I complete it. But you can draw, see if you get an Incident, then decide which option you want to use, and repeat for each Telepath.
Soval's Advanced side gives you two points for My Mind to Your Mind in addition to the reward. His second mission is Cooperation With Starfleet, and requires three deployed ships, three beamed cards with the Starfleet trait, and one Encounter in play. It rewards four points along with a Scan for Human or Starfleet, and you can send three away teams to three different locations. I love the flexibility here, but it is going to require some preparation to get the needed traits. Soval's third mission is The Needs of the Many, and requires five person cards and two Ally cards in play, with Influence at five or greater. A bit of work required, but he has a fairly person-heavy deck so this shouldn't be terribly difficult. The reward is three points and an action token, along with recalling up to two of the beamed cards and optionally logging a Duty Officer to enlist a Development. This is a fantastic reward, and is good enough to discourage me from even considering playing on the basic side. Plus, it's a thematic link to Captain Kirk's directive of the same name, with mechanical similarities with the sacrifice of a Duty Officer for a more valuable development.
↑ back to top Captain Card & Starting Components
Soval has four away teams, the Vulcan, Telepath, and Ambassador traits, and has endgame scoring for each person card that does not have the Vulcan trait. His activation lets you dismiss a Human to draw two cards, which is both mechanically valuable and a great pun. He has a Status card called the Vulcan Science Directorate. As a reaction, after you put an Anomaly or a Scientist in play, you can gain two Dilithium or a Glory. This is great because, as resources, they are pretty similar, but you can push a much quicker endgame with Soval if you want to. But watch out for that passive ability, because after you put a Time Travel card in play, you have to log this Status, because the Vulcan Science Directorate has determined that Time Travel is impossible. Prove them wrong, and they'll stop giving you Dilithium or Glory. As a note, this makes the newly introduced novelty of Krulmuth-B in Second Contact a mixed bag, because Soval loves Person cards, but has to sacrifice his Dilithium/Glory engine to get it. Is it worth it? I'll let you be the overall judge of that, but I think I'd still think twice before just handing Krulmuth-B to my opponent in any situation. The Vulcan Science Directorate may just have to finally admit that time travel is possible.
This is the first deck we've seen with no starting ship, though there is a ship hanging out in your discard pile, the Ti'Mur . The Ti'Mur shows up in an early season one episode of Enterprise to "observe" the NX-01 as they nearly kill Reed and Mayweather, using grapplers to rescue them from a collapsing ice asteroid. The Ti'Mur uses their tractor beam to rescue them, a bruise to Archer's sense of pride, while I'm left wondering why the Vulcans didn't share that piece of very useful tech. Anyway, Soval wasn't in that episode, but he does get access to the ship either the first time you shuffle the deck, or if you can pull off some Duty Officer shenanigans. The Ti'Mur has the Vulcan trait, the standard beam and warp Activations, and two deploy play actions, one where you can also discard a card to gain a military, and one that requires five Research but allows you to warp and send an away team to the warped location. I like the Vulcan ship archetype where it's sort of the nice version of the Romulans, sending their ships and warping them with an away team, but none of this Cloak nonsense.
Soval does have a controlled location, Vulcan, which has a Research skill (one of your five needed for your mission) and Influence skill and the Vulcan trait. It has the common Activation from many Available and Controlled locations that allows you to draw a card if an away team is present, and if at least three away teams are on controlled locations you can draw an extra card. With Soval's limited four away teams, you'll have to balance that with his Encounter gaining Directive (which is not Strange New Worlds) that will remove Away Teams from Controlled Locations. Vulcan also provides an extra Vulcan duty officer, which is always helpful as an option. Too bad the Ti'Mur doesn't promote Person cards.
↑ back to top Soval's Available Cards
Soval has 9 Available cards that can potentially make up his starting hand. There are three Person cards, five Directives, and an Ally. Three of his Available cards are standard, including Utilize (Directive), Recruit (Directive), and Analyze (Directive). There's also the Ally, which is a repeat of the United Earth Ally from Shran's Reserves, allowing you to scan for a Human or, take an Incident to scan two of the Ally deck. I like that this is showing up in both the Vulcan and Andorian decks, as it is representative of their early relationship.
↑ back to top Sub-Commander T'Pol (Person)
Before she was Captain Archer's First Officer and Science Officer, she worked for Soval. Her early history included work as an agent with the Ministry of Security, Deputy Science Officer under Captain Voris of the Seleya, and Ambassadorial staff for Soval at the Vulcan Embassy in San Francisco.
T'Pol gets a Research skill (two down, three to go for your first mission) and the Vulcan, Starfleet, Scientist, and Telepath traits. Thematically, I find the Telepath trait interesting as it's unclear to me if she's naturally telepathic (as many if not most Vulcans are, per the Voyager episode Counterpoint VOY: 5x10, where the Devore hunt the telepaths in their space, including Voyager's Vulcans). Anyway, she has a play action where she spends two Dilithium to scan for a card with a specific skill, which is super helpful in gaining cards with Research traits. Then, you may draw a card (not sure why you wouldn't 99% of the time) and promote a person from hand or staging area to Duty Officer, and the card specifies that this includes T'Pol. She's your primary way of promoting early on, which makes her a lynchpin for Soval's strategies. She also gets a Resupply as Duty Officer letting you gain a Dilithium or spend one Dilithium to gain one Research. Her Activation also lets you discard a Human or Engineer to draw a Cargo or Directive from your Discard pile, which I view as a great connection to Archer and Trip Tucker. I would likely use her in order to promote two Person cards, then choose to dismiss her (as Vulcan says you "may" have an additional Duty Officer with Vulcan but you can shut that off anytime) to keep her cycling through the deck. That way you get more benefits from scanning for Skill cards and promoting other Person cards as needed.
↑ back to top Stel (Person)
Stel was an investigator with the Vulcan Security Directorate investigating the bombing of the human embassy on Vulcan in 2154 until he was removed from his position after Soval discovered his connection to the terrorism (ENT 4x7: The Forge).
Stel has the Vulcan, Security, and Attack traits. His first action sends an away team to a controlled location, and you can beam a card to a deployed ship. This is compelling early on to thin your deck and get an extra card draw, so long as you have a ship to beam to. He also has a free play attack forcing your opponent to dismiss a Romulan or Andorian, and if successful, you gain a Glory. You may also log a card from your hand or discard pile, which is another bold way to speed up your deck cycling early. His Activation lets you exhaust a deployed Ship to recall a beamed card, and free play an Incident, which isn't bad at all. Against Sela, Shran, or anyone else who is playing Romulan/Andorian Duty Officers, he's a great choice for an offensive card early on with a good dose of deck-thinning. But since he's also a primary way of sending away teams, you'll need to gauge which action is better.
↑ back to top Muroc (Person)
Muroc accompanied Soval to Paan Mokar (known by the Andorians as Weytahn) to resolve a hostage crisis (ENT 2x15: Cease Fire). He has some tough decisions to make when it appears the Andorians are going back on their promises to allow Captain Archer and Soval to negotiate a ceasefire.
Muroc has a Military skill and the Vulcan trait. Muroc has a play action that allows you to choose two: gain an Influence, a Military, or send an away team to a location with a ship. If you have more than six Influence you can do all three and then log Muroc. His Activations make him the Vulcan Riker, as he can draw a Ship from your discard pile or free play a Ship with Vulcan. I often use T'Pol to promote him to Duty Officer, grab the Ti'Mur, and play it to have a ship out on the first turn. There's a situational argument for using him to gain one Influence and Military, but I'll discuss that more below.
↑ back to top Advisory (Directive)
Advisory has a free play action allowing you to spend a Dilithium to gain an Influence and draw a card, and you can beam a Human to a ship. The only early use of this would be to Beam your United Earth Ally to a Ship, then you could use Soval to dismiss United Earth to draw two cards, which would be a decent opening move. This card also has a play action that allows for a Scan 2 of the Person deck, then a self-log. It's a nice directive similar to Diplomacy but for the Vulcans.
↑ back to top Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations (Directive)
The Kol-Ut-Shan, this tentpole of Vulcan philosophy is represented via its own custom Directive and trait, Path of Surak (this trait will be used primarily in solo mode for the Soval Bot to react to). It functionally replaces Strange New Worlds as Soval's chief method for gaining Encounters. For its play action, you may remove an away team from a controlled location, log a non-Vulcan Person/Cargo/Ally from your Staging Area, and dismiss a deployed Ship. If you do all three, you get to choose between the top two Encounters, placing one on the top of your deck and the other at the bottom of the Encounter Deck. This is a slight variation on what we normally expect from Encounters, and you won't be able to use the Encounter right away. Fascinating.
↑ back to top Soval's Starting Hand Strategies
Going back to my main formula mentioned in my Georgiou guide, I first consider card draw for flexibility. There are four ways to draw a card: Activating Vulcan with an Away Team on it, free playing Advisory, playing T'Pol, and dismissing a Human. We'll go through these one by one, because they each have varying degrees of likelihood and success.
Activating Vulcan: To draw a card using this Activation, you need an Away Team. There's one way to do that: Playing Stel. And if you use him prior to deploying a ship, you'll have to waste his ability to beam. So an ideal play would theoretically be getting a ship deployed prior to playing Stel, but if you're using him to get an Away Team out you might not have that much flexibility.
Free Playing Advisory: This will cost you a Dilithium but you'll gain an Influence and be able to draw a card and beam a Human, which is what you need to be able to use Soval's Captain Activation to get two more cards. But you'll need a ship to beam that Human card to, which again presents a difficulty.
T'Pol's Play Action: T'Pol's main benefit costs two Dilithium, which you'll need Analyze's free play action or to use Utilize first, unless you spend your Glory (though Vulcan Science Directorate should get you some Dilithium or Glory back). Or unless you ignore the first line, though that's a bit inefficient. Then you can draw a card, and promote a card (probably Muroc if you can).
Soval's Dismiss a Human: So, how do we get a Human into play? The most obvious option is United Earth Ally from Soval's deck, but where do you beam it? You don't have any ships, and you'll either need Muroc as Duty Officer to grab the Ti'Mur , or else need to grab a ship with Analyze and then draw it from the top of your deck and then play it. That can work, but it will take a bit of market awareness to pull off.
The more I play with this deck, the more convinced I am that starting strategies need to center around whether or not you have T'Pol accessible. If she's in your starting hand (or you draw her using Advisory), you have far more options than if you don't. Using T'Pol to promote Muroc is the obvious move to get the Ti'Mur out of your discard and into play, but that will use two of your actions for the round (one for T'Pol, and one for the Ti'Mur ). Then you'll have drawn somewhere between 3-4 cards total, going through most of your deck. But to what benefit? I mean, yes, cycling your deck is a good thing, but you still want to make the most of your turn, and sometimes that doesn't mean cycling, it means using your actions more effectively. If the available ship in the market deck has no action cost to deploy, that may be a far better option (using Analyze) because you're also gaining points in the process of getting that ship deployed.
Now that we understand the potential to get more cards, let's shift to a discussion of whether you can use Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations (IDIC) on your first turn. You'll need an action token to play IDIC, so you only have 2 left. You'll need a Ship deployed that you can dismiss, and you'll also need an Away Team on a Planet and a Person/Cargo/Ally without Vulcan in your staging area to log. That's most likely more action tokens than you have available to you, and unless you're sacrificing United Earth you don't have the cards for the logging effect either. So, I'd look to use IDIC in turn 2 at the earliest, understanding that it's totally feasible but you need to grab some cards that you intend to play then dismiss. Get Stel played for his Away Team early, that way you get the card draw and are set up for a turn two or three IDIC.
What if you don't have T'Pol in your starting hand? I think that changes things so significantly, especially if you also don't have Stel or Advisory. If you have one of these three cards, you can at least find ways to draw more cards and search for what you need, but without all three you're a bit stuck. Obviously you can still gain cards from the Market (hopefully focusing on Human cards or ones that will help you on your missions) but that may be your main focus. Grabbing a ship, especially one with a free play operation, will really help your game because you only have two in your deck. Consider Utilize for some Dilithium gain if nothing else is appealing, and you'll be set up for a much better second turn regardless. I don't really recommend playing United Earth right away because I think that, until you get another Human, it's too useful to help you draw cards and should be used accordingly. But, maybe you want that Ally free-play so badly that it's worth it, or simply use them to gain the ally without free playing and have a three-card-gain turn. Just remember to manage that Incident-gain wisely. A promoted Stel can deal with them, but I tend to keep him out of the Duty Officer slot to thin the deck and send Away Teams as needed.
The bottom line here is that you're going to get to draw a lot of cards, especially once you start to get down into those Reserve cards. But you may not have a perfect hand to start, and so evaluating how to best set up your second turn is often the best decision. But once you get some Human cards in play, and are cycling your deck with some ships for beaming, you'll be rushing through your Reserve and Developments.
↑ back to top Soval's Reserve Cards
Soval has five reserve cards consisting of a Location, Person, Cargo, Ship and Incident. There is the normal Energy Drain Incident, which can most effectively be dealt with using an Engineer, of which Soval has none in his deck (where's Trip Tucker when you need him?).
↑ back to top Paan Mokar (Location)
"Its sole value is its strategic location near Vulcan space."
"They were setting up a military base?"
"It was the only logical conclusion."
"Was there any evidence?"
"How much evidence would you need if the Klingons decided to set up a 'colony' on Pluto?"
I love the theming around this location, because it's nearly exactly the same as the Weytahn. In fact, other than the name and a missing Imperial trait, it's exactly the same. When taking control, you may free play a Cargo or Ship. Its Activation lets you discard a card with a Weapon, Military skill, or Military focus to send an Away Team and gain an Influence. You do have to discard Paan Mokar on clean-up if you don't have away teams present, but at the end of the game you'll get four points if there are two or more Away Teams on Paan Mokar. With only four away teams, this will mean some intentionality at the endgame, but I like using Paan Mokar as the way to get card draw with Vulcan, while still cycling through away teams as you remove them for use with your IDIC Directive.
↑ back to top V'Lar (Person)
V'Lar had been a Vulcan Ambassador for almost a century when she was made the Vulcan Ambassador to Mazar (ENT 1x23: Fallen Hero). Because of the secrecy of her assignment, it was originally believed by T'Pol and the crew of the NX-01 Enterprise that she was being extradited back to Vulcan for a serious crime.
V'Lar predictably has the Vulcan and Ambassador traits. She has a Play action where you can spend Latinum to gain an Ally to your hand, presumably a reference to the corruption charges she was accused of in order to gain allies in the Mazarite society. She also has a free play action where she can take an Incident to draw a card and send an Away Team to a location where you have a ship, which as discussed is really important both for Soval's deck tempo and IDIC. Her Reaction triggers when you put an Ally in play, gaining you a drawn card, Glory, and Latinum, which is quite broad but definitely useful. Since Soval gets two Vulcan Duty Officers, she's certainly a contender.
↑ back to top Plomeek Tea (Cargo)
Those Vulcans just can't get used to human food, preferring their bland Vulcan Beverages (this card's traits) over ice cream or whatever else the NX-01 Chef can make.
This card has a free play action that lets you gain a Latinum, or a regular cost action where you can refresh your Captain and Duty Officer, and spend a Latinum to swap a card in the Junk with a card of the same suit in the market. I like the immediate flexibility here, and as we start to get weirder and weirder junk scenarios, this may have quite a bit of utility to it. Refreshing Soval would also be quite useful for card draw, making the cost of an action token more tolerable.
↑ back to top Seleya (D'Kyr) (Ship)
Presumably named for Mount Seleya, this Vulcan ship was lost in the Delphic Expanse until it was found adrift by Enterprise. Turns out, Vulcans are allergic to Trellium D, and the entire crew became zombie-like (ENT 3x5: Impulse). Soval gets pissed at Archer, claiming he didn't make a good enough effort to rescue the crew.
The Seleya has the Vulcan trait, a standard Play action which allows for deploy/warp, and discard a card to gain a Research, and a five Military required Play action where you may send an away team to the Seleya's location after deploy/warp. It has all the Activations of a normal starting ship, which is a bit of a difficult thing for Soval early on. His lack of ships can be difficult, since T'Pol is his only way to promote Person cards to Duty Officer. I think a lucky game would involve getting this ship out first, but there's not a lot of ways to guarantee that without the "Find" keyword for the Vulcan trait (Sehlat promo being the most obvious).
↑ back to top Soval's Reserve Deck Strategy
There's what we want, and what to do with what we got. I really prefer getting the Seleya first, but if beggars can't be choosers, unless you get Plomeek Tea first, everything is pretty equal when it comes to potential usefulness and won't mess with your tempo. I think there's a lot of benefit from getting V'Lar and trying to use her repeatedly for card draw and Away Teams, and the sooner you can do that the better. Paan Mokar won't hurt you as long as you can get it in play and keep it in play, the biggest issue there being the lack of weapon cards and limited Military focus/skills (except Muroc) in your Available cards. You can decide to use T'Pol to scan for Military, which would help with that, but the Andorians still have it easier. Plomeek Tea isn't terrible, I just don't see myself using it for the Play Action unless I really need something from the junk, in which case the cost is effectively two action tokens (one for the Tea and one to grab from the market).
I'll also note that by the time we get into these Reserve cards, card draw is no longer an issue. We'll likely have enough away teams out for consistent double card draw from Vulcan, and we'll be consistently getting more card draw through T'Pol and V'Lar without even trying. So don't fear - the worry of getting exactly what you need on your first and second turn should be quickly alleviated once you're into the Reserves.
↑ back to top Soval's Development Cards
Soval has six Development cards, including two Persons, one Cargo, one Location, and two Directives.
↑ back to top T'Pau (Person) (Dev Cost 2 Glory)
From the Season 4 Vulcan Unrest arc of Enterprise (ENT 4x7-9) and Amok Time (TOS 2x5), T'Pau is a member of the Syrranites, a non-violent faction of Vulcans who find mind-melds acceptable (because apparently mainstream Vulcans didn't for thousands of years) and the Vulcan Council's war on Andoria repugnant.
T'Pau has the Vulcan and Telepath traits, and gains two wild skills when she is Duty Officer. Her Play action lets you draw a card, and both players may return an Incident from their hand or discard piles, and she gains a Glory for each returned Incident. You can then promote her or another Person from hand or Staging Area to Duty officer. She does cost two Glory, but her value is slightly above average at four points (plus that Play action can be Glory generating). I'll be honest, T'Pau is not necessarily my first choice among the Developments unless we're getting into the late game and I'm in desperate need of some Incident management. Soval doesn't have Doctor cards so it can be necessary, especially if Soval is stuck with some difficult to return Incident. She can also speed up the end of the game with her play action, so there's certainly a place for her. Late in the game, I'm not usually looking for wild skills but it if turns out that I need to hit a threshold for Vulcan Hello, it might be worth it.
↑ back to top Minister Kuvak (Person) (Dev Cost 2 Latinum)
Soval's ally on the Vulcan High Council, Kuvak was played by John Rubinstein who ironically played the Mazarite captain hunting down V'Lar on a prior season of Enterprise. He is noticeably skeptical of intelligence suggesting the Andorians had obtained a version of the Xindi weapon to use against Vulcan, and was troubled bythe decision by Administrator V'Las to invade Andoria in a preemptive strike.
Kuvak gets the Vulcan trait and a free play action that gets you a Dilithium for every card beamed to a Location or a Ship. He also has a passive ability that increases your hand size for every non-Vulcan controlled location in play (up to 3). I think that's where he shines, because you will hopefully still have Utilize so you won't need his play action as often. The catch here is that using IDIC repeatedly may mean you don't have a lot of Away Teams out in the Neutral Zone, so you may not control that many planets. Still, he's easy to develop (V'Lar and Plomeek Tea both give Latinum) and will help you with a large late game hand size.
↑ back to top Kir'Shara (Cargo) (Dev Cost 2 Dilithium, 1 Latinum, and dismiss a Duty Officer)
The Kir'Shara was the ancient Vulcan relic that had the original teachings of Surak, which had been lost for centuries. When Captain Archer mind-melds with a Syrrannite containing the Katra of Surak, he goes on a mission to find the Kir'Shara and prevent the looming Vulcan invasion of Andoria and potential Vulcan civil war.
The Kir'Shara gets the Path of Surak trait. The free Play Action applies if you have seven Vulcan cards in play (excluding Captain Soval), and lets you take an Incident and the top Encounter before logging Kir'Shara. It scores for Research Focus. Gaining another Encounter is always great, it's just an issue of whether you have seven Vulcan cards in play once you get it. If you have the opportunity to develop, use it, and log it, it's a no-brainer.
↑ back to top Mount Seleya (Location) (Dev Cost 2 Latinum and 1 or more Vulcan Logged)
A religious site for the Vulcans, it appears in Star Trek III, IV and Enterprise, and was the site of a battle between the Vulcans and "those who marched beneath the raptor's wings", presumably the Vulcans who left to become the Romulans. It has significant spiritual significance to the Vulcan people, and is where Leonard McCoy brought Spock's Katra after they found a resurrected Spock on the Genesis planet.
Mount Seleya has the Vulcan and Transcendent traits, and a Research and Influence Skill. Its Control action lets you draw a card for each Vulcan in your Log, which kind of works in opposition to the goals of the Kir'Shara, unless you get Kir'Shara first and start logging cards after. Its activation is amazing though, letting you find any card at the discard cost of a person card. At the end of the game, it scores a point for each Telepath in play, including your Captain, so at least a point. But again, that can run counter to the goal of logging Vulcans.
↑ back to top Vulcan High Command (Directive) (Dev Cost 3 Dilithium)
"I've dealt with the High Command. Vulcans can lie and cheat with the best of them." Jonathan Archer. The High Command oversaw Vulcan military and civilian organizations, having the power to control their space fleet, scientific institutions, and foreign relations, including dismissing Soval as Ambassador right before the invasion of Andoria.
A mirror of Shran's Andorian Commandos, this Directive gets the Vulcan trait and scores for Military Focus. Its free Play action lets you send an away team to a Location where you have a Ship, and find a Vulcan. Its regular Play action lets you scan for a Starfleet, draw two cards, then self-log Vulcan High Command. I love the theme here, as Soval is dismissed from the High Command after allying himself with Starfleet to warn the Andorians of a preemptive strike based on bad military intelligence (lots of thinly veiled American military analogies during Enterprise's run, precisely because Trek tends to comment on current events through a fictional lens). As far as mechanisms, that Play action is really great for Soval, especially when he's looking for a particular Vulcan card at any given moment. I would try to get this played for its free play at least once or twice prior to logging it.
↑ back to top Vulcan Hello (Directive) (Dev Cost 4 Dilithium)
It's not just the hand greeting (live long and prosper), it's also how the Vulcans deal with the Klingons (DIS 1x1: The Vulcan Hello). Live long and prosper… and also Photon Torpedo Target Locked.
This custom Directive has the Attack and Communication traits, and two Military skills. Its standard Attack play action lets you dismiss an opponent's ship at a neutral location, and send an away team to the same location, and gain a Glory if your opponent has a Klingon in play. The free action requires nine military and you can log the Vulcan Hello to select a neutral location not secured by your opponent, and take control. This is incredibly powerful considering that you don't need to have it secured either (unlike Picard's Make it So). It scores two points. I think that if you happen to have some Klingons you're going up against (which will be common in Georgiou v. Soval games), this has a nice bonus, but the real icing on the cake here is using Vulcan Hello to take anything your opponent hasn't secured. Just make sure you plan for it with Military as Soval tends to favor Influence early on. Using Vulcan Hello's Attack play action with T'Pau as Duty Officer is four Military skills right there, so you've got the kit to reach the nine military threshold if you plan for it.
↑ back to top Soval's Development Strategy
As always, development strategy is highly dependent on your game state, current tempo, and how close you are to the end of the game. I personally think that with only six developments, it's highly likely that you'll know exactly which development to take without really consulting this guide. But let's consider a couple scenarios for a moment.
If your Reserve isn't empty (because you used Ancient Relay Station or you completed Needs of the Many mission), then I suggest Vulcan High Command or Minister Kuvak. This is because Vulcan High Command provides constant Away Teams along with control over the Vulcans you can Find, including ones left in your Reserve deck. Kuvak is a fine second choice because of the potential for a large hand size, but make sure you're actually controlling non-Vulcan locations if that's the case. It would be silly to get him and promote him without anything to show for it.
If you're in desperate need of some Influence or Military for restriction requirements, the T'Pau makes a lot of sense. She's also my go-to if I'm dealing with a lot of Incidents, especially if we're approaching the end of the game and I want to finish it on my own terms. Another potential if the game is rushing to the end is Kir'Shara, but you have to make sure you have the Vulcan traits in play to make it worth it. Thankfully, it's a free play, and Soval has twelve total Vulcan traits in his deck to draw from, but still, be aware, especially if you used Stel to log cards early on.
Speaking of logging cards early on, Mount Seleya is a contradiction in a couple ways. You might want to get it out for the excellent Activation, but the earlier you do the less likely you are to have the Vulcan traits logged. That is, unless you're logging Vulcan traits with Stel early in the game. But then, do you have enough for the Kir'Shara? And if you're logging Vulcans, does the Endgame for this card (Telepaths in play) matter that much? The ideal play would be to get Kir'Shara played first with all the Vulcans needed, then start logging Vulcans without Telepath like crazy. A high order, especially for the end of the game, but if you can pull it off, it's great. But you could also just log one or two Vulcan traits, play Mount Seleya, and then repeatedly use the Activation to find cards you want. Technically it would be another good card to get out early as a Development to get through the rest of your Reserves, which would make missing out on the Control ability less of a big deal since you're avoiding multiple trips through the Reserve Deck.
Last but not least, there's some timing considerations for Vulcan Hello. Normally, I'd say this one isn't as much of a priority. However, against a super aggressive opponent, it might be necessary. Dismissing your opponent's ships and sending an Away Team isn't always necessary, but you might need to slow your opponent's Neutral Zone progress, and if so this is your best non-market weapon.

↑ back to top Person Deck

Second Contact: I think the best card for Soval here is D'Erika Tendi simply based on her ability to gain a Ship from the junk. I think Jennifer Sh'Reyan's Support operation is also pretty good for Soval because he'll be gaining a lot of Person cards and then sending an Away Team to a Controlled location is exactly what his deck is doing. And Parmen is pretty awesome for everyone but Soval certainly benefits too, and he can easily hit the six Influence restriction of Parmen's Reaction.

Necheyev is nearly always worth it for me, and as a Human/Starfleet with the ability to repeat a discarded Directive and an Influence Focus, Soval is particularly benefited by her. Finally, consider Kamarag if you need to Scan for Starfleet.

Second Contact: Moopsy looks like it would be a great card for Soval, and it might be after you've used Kir'Shara. But before that point, it feels like a trap for your Vulcan traits. Be wise if you let the Moopsy out. Soval can probably benefit from Protocol 12 but you'll need to find some more Doctors. Core Set: Tribbles only really matters against Klingon Duty Officers, but watch for chances to use it. Tachyon Detection Grid continues to just be a silver bullet against a straightforward Sela game, but it's nothing special for Soval. ↑ back to top Individual Captain Considerations
Here's some thoughts on how Soval stacks up against each captain so far, and some general principles I'd apply when facing them:
↑ back to top To Boldly Go
Georgiou: Watch out for her in the Neutral Zone, as she's going to be controlling more than you early on unless you can slow her down with some ships of your own. She's got a bit more dynamic developments than Soval, so push in where you can and get Encounters often, leveraging them to mess with her game. Don't be afraid to speed the game up using the Vulcan Science Directorate. Her game is going to be quick (at least against an experienced player) so you might want to lean in on Stel and a logging-heavy strategy.
Kirk: Rushing the game against Kirk might win out, it might not, but both of you want lots of Encounters which will mean a race to get the best ones. When not combining encounter markets, keep that in mind (a card on the bottom will come up again). Kirk has a lot of ships and the ability to move up his tracks. Your best move will be to match him in the Neutral Zone while beating him to endgame scoring. Don't let him set everything up, even if it means sacrificing Kir-Shara or Vulcan Hello. You can beat him in a quick game, but prolonged he might wear you down.
Archer: If you can get some of Archer's crew from the main deck, do it. They help you as both Person and Starfleet cards. Unfortunately, he'll be able to scan for them with his NX-01 scan, so you should consider using United Earth to scan for a human and hope you pull one of his crew. Likewise, use your T'Pol to scan for what you need before Archer can get his T'Pol out of the Reserves and do the same. A Research Skill scan in the person deck is worth beating him to the punch. You also have a chance to fight with him in the Neutral Zone, either peacefully controlling planets or preventing each other from taking anything.
Rebner: You will probably lose the Neutral Zone fight to Rebner, so don't worry too much about it. Instead, focus on cards that defend you from attacks, if you can get them. Gain or Take any Military focus scoring you can find, as that's all Rebner cares about for track multipliers. His game can go quickly but don't be so worried about the rush that you don't set up a dynamic last few turns.
Khan: I would seek out cards that help you return Incidents, and get T'Pau as soon as possible to avoid a burn loss. Likewise, I think you should focus on scanning for Sarina Douglas (Blue Research scan is best) as soon as possible to deprive Khan of her trait.
↑ back to top Second Contact
Pike: I don't recommend trying to initiate a Burn (like I did with Georgiou) because I think that Soval can get stuck with Incidents himself. I also don't recommend grabbing Time Travel cards from Pike (unless you just want to throw away Vulcan Science Directorate early). Instead, understand that Pike has a dynamic toolkit and look for opportunities to mess with it. He also has a lot of Person cards and not a lot of ships, so fighting to get ideal Ships for a neutral zone fight is a pretty good strategy.
Riker: Just like Georgiou, you'll be locked in a competition for Research/Military. While Riker can pivot to Research, he does it via Military traits, so try to get military skills from the market and Neutral Zone early. Otherwise, you'll make it too easy for him to move up both tracks, while preventing yourself from maxing out Military.
Freeman : I would try to get Vulcan Hello as soon as possible against Freeman in order to mess with her priorities in getting Ships out. Dismissing a few ships here and there will slow down her game and give you a leg up. Likewise, rush Glory before she can fully enlist her developments.
↑ back to top Core Box
Picard: Depriving Picard of synthetics can work, especially if you decide to scan for and obtain Bruce Maddox before he does. You're both going after different decks (Person versus Ally) but it can't hurt to deprive him of some specific Alien traits when you see them. Klingons don't do you any favors so Junk them before Picard can benefit. And watch out for a very fast playing Picard, and adjust accordingly.
Shran: The best thematic match-up, you're going to get attacked by Talas and you'll be attacking with Stel. Keep that in mind as you shift to finding non-Vulcan cards to use to avoid those attacks, and they do the same with Andorians. Junking or taking weapons will help you with Paan Mokar and take those same weapons away from Shran. And you'll both be rushing to grab ships, so make it count.
Koloth: Advice against Koloth is nearly identical to Georgiou playing Koloth: He can rush endgame quicker than anyone else, so your move will have to be making the most of the turns you have. Koloth's pretty good at messing with the neutral zone and attacking, so focus on your strengths in moving up tracks and beaming or logging everything. You'll also be fighting for Military focus cards, so be aware and grab them first. Consider not going after Kir'Shara but using Vulcan Hello to take Glory and give him a taste of his own medicine. Don't forget to grab ships early and often.
Sisko: He's going to get more ships than you, most likely, but you can still focus on grabbing the best ones for your game early on. Scanning Military Skills for the K'T'Inga will take away valuable skills and focus scoring, though you risk getting the D'Kora Marauder instead. Don't worry too much about the location fights with Sisko, but look for opportunities to speed the game up when you can. A smart Sisko player won't have any trouble with cycling and card draw, but don't make it easy on them. I would suggest limiting games to ten turns or less when you can, and choose your priorities according to the somewhat shorter game.
Sela: While the Scimitar is a beast, you've got your own weapon with Vulcan Hello. You're not going to be able to take locations without focused attention on the neutral zone, so it might be worth it to send Away Teams to the Neutral Zone instead of your controlled locations. But don't sacrifice card draw simply to stall out against an opponent that will overwhelm you with ships and Away Teams. Turn your attention to grabbing cards for points, completing missions quickly, and gaining Glory however you can. You can rush Sela and prevent her from total control, just don't let her get to her Developments too quickly via card shenanigans like Ancient Relay Station. That may be worth fighting over.
Burnham: I've never had a particularly long or low scoring Burnham game, but you can try to keep up by making sure you're besting her at focus scoring and grabbing every Research and Influence skill and focus you can. Beyond that, making sure not to give her easy Dilithium gain will slow her a bit, especially when it comes to Developments. Unfortunately, she can fuel her own track-gain with the skills on cards she already has. And while she wants ships too, hers are going to be from the junk so maybe just try not to junk too many ships.
↑ back to top Putting It All Together
I think that T'Pol is the clear early game lynchpin of any successful game with Soval. She promotes and scans for necessary skill cards, making her invaluable to play over and over again. As such, getting a ship out that other cards can be beamed to for a thin deck to play her repeatedly is important. Her ability requires knowledge of market conditions, making high-level Soval play dependent on knowing what a scan will reveal.
The Vulcan Science Directorate presents an interesting conundrum. Avoiding Time Travel traits altogether is not that difficult, but it will close off an entire area of strategy (recalling cards) that you can benefit immensely from. It also leaves those cards for your opponents. I think in the circumstance where you can junk them easily, it's worth taking the Time Travel cards off the table for everyone, but remember, logging the Vulcan Science Directorate doesn't end your game, it simply takes one more tool out of your toolkit. Low on Dilithium or want to speed up the game? It's worth building a strategy around but beyond that, it's not critical. If Krumuth B is in the Neutral Zone and you have a chance of actually taking it, that's not a bad idea. All things in balance, and don't be too stubborn to admit what we all know - Time Travel is real.
Soval has to balance his attacks with working towards actually accomplishing his goals of a high score and gaining Encounters. This is a tough balance with an aggressive enemy, but if Soval plays passively he's going to get overrun, both in terms of the Neutral Zone and with frequent dismissal of Duty Officers. Finding some defensive cargo would be great, although you may be subject to the whims of the market. If worst comes to worst, use Vulcan Hello aggressively.
Soval's missions require some pre-planning, but not a ton. You're already going after Person, Starfleet, and Ally cards simply due to the nature of the kit, and as long as you do find some Research and wild skills early you'll be set up for a quick mission completion. I also suggest trying to get The Needs of the Many done quickly so you have something from your Developments as soon as possible, then just figure out what's most useful in the moment. I don't see resource management as being terribly difficult for this deck so you should have a choice in which one to take. Your biggest early issues will get solved with some ships and ability to promote duty officers via the Seleya. Gaining a few more ships and trying to find opportunities for IDIC will push this deck above opponents.
Thematically, the progression of Soval from Vulcan Ambassador to the Federation to full-fledged supporter of the Syrrannites and the original teachings of Surak is very present in the deck. It even gives you the choice in the developments, whether to focus on Surak at Mount Seleya via the Kir'Shara, or be more militant with Vulcan Hello and Vulcan High Command. But I'm looking forward to seeing how well Soval interacts with the market. I had a game recently where he recruited Hoshi, Mayweather, and Reed and it felt like they worked well with his kit, for a thematic reenactment of the finale to Kir'Shara.
