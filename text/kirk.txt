Introduction
It's the Captain who started it all, Captain James Tiberius Kirk, specifically, Admiral/Captain Kirk from Star Trek II-VI. The choice to use the movie version of Kirk was a good one, for a couple reasons. First, it pairs well thematically against the The Wrath of Khan version of Khan. Second, I would argue that movie-era Kirk is equally recognizable to the general public, if not more so, than the Kirk from the Original Series. Third, and I think most importantly, this deck works so well mechanically with the custom directives that are straight out of The Wrath of Khan and The Search for Spock. I also love that this is a 4/10 complexity deck, because it has some specific areas of complexity that fans of the game will enjoy while not being impossible for the new players who really just want to play with Kirk. Though if it's the first game for both players, please don't play Kirk versus Khan first. Please don't. I want you all to like this game, not get disillusioned by a terrible first play.

Basic Side
Advanced Side
Kirk's player board's basic side track multipliers max out at 5x for all skill tracks, all at the 10th space of the tracks. As always the advanced side's thresholds are one less. His basic mission is Where No Man Has Gone Before, which is a perfect reference to Star Trek's opening credits mission statement. It requires Kirk to have two Encounters on the same ship, and the Research track at four or higher. This means you won't be able to immediately play an ongoing Encounter like Guardian of Forever without losing the ability to beam it to a ship in the future. But, if you get the new Gomtuu Encounter-Ship, and have an Encounter beamed to Gomtuu, that would be two Encounters on the same ship. Point being, when you do get Ongoing Encounters, consider this mission and how to accomplish it with the Encounters you happen to gain before immediately using them. Once you accomplish his mission, you gain another Encounter from the top of the Encounter deck, and you remove two Glory from the Stardate card, thus accelerating the end of the game.
Kirk's Advanced side gives you four points for Where No Man Has Gone Before in addition to the Reward. His second mission is The Undiscovered Country, a reference to the movie of the same name. This one's a bit easier, only requiring three of the same species trait (excluding Starfleet/Human cards) in play and four or more Influence. It rewards you with a point and you can enlist a Development for free. Kirk's third mission is called Search For Spock, also thematically referencing the Star Trek movie of the same name. You'll need to log Captain Spock, have the H.M.S. Bounty in play, five persons in play, and Military at four or more. That requires a bit of finesse and focus, but Kirk's deck has a lot of Person cards. Completing it will give you four points, allow you to redraw Captain Spock from your log and free Play him. Then you may find an Incident and return it, gain an action token, and are required to Dismiss the H.M.S. Bounty . This mission has some great rewards, and honestly you might even end up completing the Advanced Missions before Where No Man Has Gone Before.
↑ back to top Captain Card & Starting Components
Kirk has five away teams along with the Human and Starfleet traits. His Reaction allows you to gain a Military after putting a card with the Klingon trait in play, because he's never trusted Klingons, and he never will. Alternatively, you can use his Activation to put a card on top of your deck to draw a Directive from your Discard pile, which allows for some decent flexibility in, say, gaining Utilize or Strange New Worlds back for repeated use (or the Time Warp Directive as seen in the Developments). Kirk also gets Endgame Scoring of a point for every second step gained on your lowest Skill track at the end of the game. So, if the lowest track was two Research, you'd get a point, up to fourteen or more on all three tracks, which would be worth seven points.
Kirk starts with the U.S.S. Enterprise Refit , which is exactly what you would expect from a "starting Enterprise " - essentially a copy of Picard's Enterprise-D . Kirk also gets to start with a controlled location in Starfleet Headquarters. Famously shown in San Francisco, it gives Kirk another wild skill and the Starfleet trait. There's no Activation text here (so no standard Farpoint/Andoria/Bajor style card cycling), but there is a special text that instructs you to send a number of Away Teams equal to the number on the Stardate card to one location when you log Starfleet Headquarters. This will be less strong in Cadet Mode, but generally speaking there's a good incentive to wait for the away teams (and to use the wild skill), but an equal incentive to log early to get an encounter.
↑ back to top Kirk's Available Cards
Kirk has 10 Available cards: three Person cards, five Directives, one Cargo, and one Incident. All five Directives are familiar, including Utilize, Set a Course, Maximum Warp, Recruit, and Strange New Worlds. It's great that Kirk has early access to Strange New Worlds to attempt an early Encounter, and he gets quite a bit of free warp capabilities and card draw from Maximum Warp and Set a Course. You also have Subspace Phenomenon, which returns more potently with a Time Travel trait in play or by discarding a card with Research Focus or Skill.
↑ back to top Nyota Uhura (Person)
The long-standing communications officer of the U.S.S. Enterprise, played by Nichelle Nichols. Her movie exploits include fake dying in the Kobayashi Maru simulation, illegally beaming Kirk and company to the Enterprise to aid in its theft, stealing Uranium protons in 20th Century San Francisco, and helping expose a conspiracy to sabotage peace negotiations between the Federation and the Klingons. Also, that weird erotic dance with the fans…
Uhura gets an Influence skill with the Human, Starfleet, and Communication (duh) traits. She has two Actions, both of which cost an action token. The first gives Kirk the ability to gain Person or Ally cards for two Dilithium. The second requires five Influence, and lets you gain an Alien in the market or the Junk. Her Activation lets you spend up to three Dilithium for Glory so long as you have three different non-Starfleet species in play. I like the potential here, and it works thematically well with gaining Aliens in order to gain an Encounter with Universal Translator from the To Boldly Go Cargo Deck and helps you complete The Undiscovered Country mission.
↑ back to top Leonard McCoy (Person)
He's a Doctor, not a Strategema expert. As Kirk's confidant and the Chief Medical Officer on board the Enterprise (Refit and A), Leonard "Bones" McCoy, oversaw the casualties of the Battle of the Mutara Nebula. He subsequently became the carrier of Spock's Katra, insisting on going back to the Genesis planet and subsequently Vulcan to reunite the Katra with Spock's resurrected body. He got to berate some 20th century doctors for drilling holes in their patient's heads while saving an older lady from kidney failure in a detour from kidnapping some whales. And of course, he accompanied Jim Kirk at Rura Penthe after a show trial convicted Kirk and Bones of assassinating the Klingon Ambassador Gorkon, getting rescued in time to do some surgery on a torpedo. One thing's for certain: despite his protestations, he's more than just a Doctor.
Leonard McCoy has the Human, Doctor, and Starfleet traits, and uses his action to gain a Research and return an Incident. His Reaction lets you free play an Incident from your hand or discard pile when you discard or log a Person during your action step. That's a pretty flexible ability considering Spock's play action centers around logging cards. Still, I like returning Incidents more than Free Playing them typically, so I'll likely keep Bones moving through the deck.
↑ back to top Captain Spock (Person)
Promoted to Captain prior to the events of The Wrath of Khan in 2285, Spock was officially in charge of the Enterprise for its use as a training vessel for cadets, but conceded command to Admiral Kirk during the Genesis Device Incident. His logical Vulcan demeanor and the necessity to repair the warp drive resulted in the death of Spock in 2285. Fortunately, he was resurrected on the Genesis Planet, went through rapid growth, and was reconnected with his Katra at Mount Seleya on Vulcan. He then proceeded to talk to some Whales and negotiate peace with the Klingons (not to mention establish the first steps towards Romulan and Vulcan reunification).
Spock has a slew of traits, both Human and Vulcan, as well as Telepath, Scientist, and Starfleet. He also has a Research skill. His Play Action lets you log a card from your hand or discard pile to gain three Dilithium, and if the card has a skill you can move up the associated track (choosing if multiple or wild). Spock's Reaction triggers when gaining a Person card, at which point you can spend a Dilithium to send an Away Team where you have a ship and then gain a Glory. Spock is great for an early-game logging strategy, though many players will struggle to know who to use him on. You'll likely want to log Uhura or McCoy, but probably not both, then maybe start getting rid of some directives. Alternatively you can focus on sending Away Teams with Spock as Duty Officer, which you'll need to do a lot of if you intend to get Strange New Worlds played early and often.
↑ back to top Levitation Boots (Cargo)
I'll admit that the first time I saw this cargo, I mistakenly thought it was from Star Trek VI, The Final Frontier, as there were anti-gravity boots worn by Ambassador Gorkon's Starfleet Assassins (the hunt for the boots becomes a major plot point in the investigation). However, these aren't those boots. These are the boots that Spock wears while looking for Kirk as he's free-climbing El Capitan in Star Trek V: The Final Frontier. On a humorous note, they make a referential appearance in Lower Decks as well (wej duj).
Levitation Boots is a free play card that deploys to your fleet area. You can then beam up to three Person cards from your hand or discard pile, though you'd obviously prefer discard pile. While you can beam more Person cards from your hand at the cost of a Dilithium, you'll more often be recalling a Person card beamed to Levitation Boots. If you ever have no cards beamed here, you'll dismiss the card. I'll talk about the strategy here a little more in the next section, but I am personally a fan of using this whenever you have access to the most Person cards in your discard pile. This lets you thin your deck without sacrificing actions. There's also some strategy to simply using this to "rescue" a Person from your discard that you want to play that turn. I would go so far as to say that managing Levitation Boots is a crucial element to any Kirk game.
↑ back to top Kirk's Starting Hand Strategies
Kirk starts out with some of the best card-draw options in the game. Between Set a Course and Maximum Warp, you can potentially see a total of 3 additional cards in your deck. You also have the potential to use Subspace Phenomenon to discard Spock and then draw two cards and discard one, which if you're keeping track, is your whole starting deck. Let's game that out for a second, and I'll note that the first time I did this I made the error of giving my self too many cards (credit to Juxtapoze on Discord for messaging me and pointing this out). So, here's my second attempt at a magic Christmas land for a turn one encounter while also burning through your deck:
You have Spock, Maximum warp, Set a Course, Subspace Phenomenon, and one other card in your starting hand. You play Subspace Phenomenon, discarding Spock, and draw two cards and discard one of them. You NEED to keep Levitation Boot if possible, and you hope that you've discarded Uhura or Bones as the discarded card. Your hand is now Maximum Warp, Set a Course, Levitation Boots (for this to work), and one other card. Then, you play Maximum Warp, which lets you discard any card, not just the one you drew. You warp the U.S.S. Enterprise Refit to Starfleet HQ, and you discard the other Person card (either Bones or Uhura) that you either had before or drew in the last turn. Now you've got one card in your deck and 3 cards in your discard, with at least two Persons (hopefully 3) and levitation boots hopefully in your hand. At this point, if you don't have those cards, you can't pull this off, and really that last card needs to be Utilize or Recruit (if it's Strange New Worlds this won't work either). At this point, you beam all 3 persons using Levitation Boots' free play action. Now you have an empty discard, Maximum Warp in your Staging area, and you have 2 of either SNW, Utilize, or Recruit in your hand with the other in the deck, and Set A Course still in your hand from before. It's possible you only have two cards in your deck since the other card you discarded using Subspace Phenomenon could be one of the Directives, which could mean your last Person card is in the deck. Now, you can recall Spock from Levitation Boots and promote him, and place Set A Course on top of your deck to gain a Person via Recruit, sending an Away Team to Starfleet Headquarters. but that one card in your deck MUST be Utilize or Set a Course for this to work. Now you should have one card left in your hand, Strange New Worlds, and you can gain a turn one Encounter with a very thin starting deck, and ironically enough a wasted Action token unless you can use it on your Encounter that you gained, which would be satisfying.
The more straightforward way for a turn one Encounter is to warp the Enterprise (either via Maximum warp or its Activation), sending an Away Team with Set a Course (discarding at least one Person), and playing Strange New Worlds. It costs 2 action tokens and if you've got those three cards in your starting hand, it's more reliable than my "burn through the deck magic-Christmas-land" scenario.
Let's go the opposite direction. What if you don't have any cards in your starting hand that give you the ability to draw more cards? All is not actually lost, as you can set up for a nearly equally dynamic second turn by making sure you end the round with all three of your Person cards in the discard pile (and you'll be able to just beam them to Levitation boots in round two from your discard). Alternatively, you can focus on gaining some Dilithium with Utilize and using it to power Uhura's Play action. You might even consider using Spock to log Analyze in order to simply rely on Uhura for gaining Person or Ally cards.
Should you choose to use Set a Course to send three Away Teams? Possibly. It depends on how militant your opponent is. If you're not going to control any locations it's probably not worth it to send all three, but you might consider sending two Person cards, especially if you also have Levitation boots in hand. I think that if I had Levitation Boots, all three starting Persons, and Set a Course, I would probably send two away teams and discard Spock and McCoy, then beam them to Levitation Boots, recall Spock, use Uhura and my starting Glory to gain a card, and potentially use Spock to log that card from my discard pile to gain some Dilithium and track movement, finishing by warping the Enterprise to the location I sent Away Teams to potentially Control the following turn. It's not the most efficient use of the cards, but it's not terrible either. You can use the same strategy with Recruit to gain an Ally and take an Incident, and depending on what you get you may have some options there too (especially with Spock and another Subspace Phenomenon).
What's the worst opening? I think McCoy, Strange New Worlds, Subspace Phenomenon, Levitation Boots, and either Recruit or Utilize. And there's still a workable first hand there, as you can still get a benefit from your card-gaining directive, use McCoy to return an Incident, and potentially beam a gained Person card to Levitation Boots. There's always a way out of a bad starting hand in this game, and it usually involves opening up your options with card draw or gaining Incidents to use for paying discarding costs (in order to beam other cards)
↑ back to top Kirk's Reserve Cards
Kirk has five Reserve Cards consisting of two Directives, two Person Cards, and a Ship. One of the Directives is Analyze, which we've seen before and is the basic Directive for gaining Ships, Cargo or some free Dilithium. The other Reserve cards are new:
↑ back to top Needs of the Many (Directive)
An oft-cited Vulcan (and Human) philosophical statement, uttered most directly in connection with Spock's sacrifice in Wrath of Khan, appears as a thematic free-play Directive. You'll dismiss a deployed ship or Duty Officer, then log that dismissed card to enlist a person from your Developments. I like to use this thematically to log Spock (or someone else), though you could also use it to Log the U.S.S. Enterprise Refit i in an attempt to get the U.S.S. Enterprise-A developed for free. Needs of the Many does self-log, so you'll only get to do it once (without some trick like Memory Alpha, which isn't in To Boldly Go but may show up if you're mixing Advanced Locations).
↑ back to top Pavel Chekov (Person)
Chekov goes through quite the evolution in the movie-era, from First Officer of the Reliant to Khan's controlled minion, he escapes mind control and lives to find the Nuclear Wessels. He also gets to put his investigative skills to work, because if the boots don't fit, you must acquit.
The first appearance of Mr. Chekov in To Boldly Go, he has the Ops, Attack, Human, and Starfleet traits. His Play Action requires you discard the top card of your deck to send an Away Team to a location, and if your opponent already has an Away Team there they discard a card. Chekov's reaction triggers upon warping a ship to a neutral location, and if you discard a Person card you can send an Away Team and gain a Glory. I prefer to use Chekov as Duty Officer for that very common reaction trigger, but if you've got an aggressive Away Team focused opponent, this can be a really good card for stealing a secured location before it can be controlled.
↑ back to top Montgomery "Scotty" Scott (Person)
A ship full of cadets needs a good leader, and Scotty inspires them all to stay at their post. Constantly working to undo the damage caused to the Enterprise, he exaggerates his repair schedule just enough to make the Captain believe he's a miracle worker. From sabotaging the Excelsior to maybe inventing transparent Aluminum, Scotty transfers over to the Enterprise-A and keeps her running for two more movies. He's giving her all she's got.
Scotty is the ever vigilant Chief Engineer of the Enterprise, earning his Human, Starfleet, and Engineer Traits. He has a Free Play action that lets you find a ship, and if it's found in your reserve deck (the H.M.S. Bounty ), you take an Incident. This can absolutely be worth it to speed up your tempo, but even if you're just fishing Ships out of your deck that's helpful. His other Play Action lets you dismiss a deployed ship to trigger a controlled location's control operation and draw a card. Like Riker before him, abilities that let you re-use control operations can be extremely powerful, making Scotty a multipurpose card to be used liberally. But that's not all, as he also has an Activation that gives you two Dilithium and allows for a Ship or Cargo refresh. While a ship may be the obvious choice, refreshing certain Cargo can be absolutely overpowered and situationally makes him an excellent Duty Officer as well as all-around great card. Don't log Scotty. Let him stew in a transporter buffer for 75 years or so.
↑ back to top H.M.S. Bounty (Ship)
A reference to the 18th Century Royal Navy ship taken from its Captain by mutineers, it was Dr. McCoy's suggestion for a new name for the B'rel class Klingon Bird-of-Prey taken over by the crew in The Search For Spock, though it was not named as such until The Voyage Home.
The H.M.S. Bounty has the cloak and Klingon traits, along with two free play actions. The first is a simple deploy action, while the second requires five Influence and lets you discard an incident to send an Away Team to any location, with the standard "ignoring any opponent Ship" text typical of cards with the Cloak trait. You can also warp and beam with the standard Activations. This ship will also consistently set off Kirk's reaction, where he can discard a card to gain a military.
↑ back to top Kirk's Reserve Deck Strategy
There's not a lot to discuss here, though it's worth noting that if you can get the H.M.S. Bounty out of your reserves using Scotty, you should do so (it's worth taking the Incident). I like to use Needs of the Many as soon as possible with Spock when I can, just to get a Person from the Developments quicker. Beyond that, you can work with whatever the fates hand you from the Reserve Deck. Just keep that deck thin and cycle away. Alternatively, getting Scotty as a duty officer allows you to avoid using Utilize and continue refreshing Levitation Boots or refreshing Ships for continuous beaming and warping. There is a question of how important Analyze is at all. Yes, a couple Dilithium can be helpful, but you don't need a ton of ships (especially once you consider your Developments). Your biggest concern is using it to grab cargo, if you need it. It's probably worth not logging it in case you need something important from the Cargo deck.
↑ back to top Kirk's Development Cards
Kirk has seven Development cards, including two Persons, two Ships, one Location, and two Directives.
↑ back to top Hikaru Sulu (Person) (Dev Cost 2 Dilithium)
Oh my! It's the Enterprise helmsman, and the Captain of the Excelsior. Sulu's movie exploits include piloting the U.S.S. Enterprise Refit (I don't think these kids can steer), flying a Huey 204 Helicopter, and targeting explosions and firing.
Sulu has the Pilot, Human, and Starfleet traits in addition to a Military skill. His Play Action has you Take an Incident to your discard pile, and then send two Away Teams to a location where you have a ship. It's a slightly different version of what we see from Worf, without the discard from the top of your deck and of course you need a ship to send those Away Teams. His reaction lets you ignore the negative effect of an attack by exhausting a Ship, which is very versatile and makes me want to leave him as Duty Officer. He scores two points.
↑ back to top Sarek (Person) (Dev Cost 1 Dilithium and 1 Latinum)
Father of Spock, we've seen him before in this box, though this is the Mark Lenard version. That being said, the text on these two cards is nearly identical, with only the word "Ambassador" differentiating them. It's cool to see how these cards can mechanically benefit both decks, while remaining mechanically consistent.
Sarek still has an Influence skill with the Vulcan, Telepath, and Ambassador traits. He also has the same exact Play Action as his Burnham deck counterpart, gaining 1 Influence for each Vulcan in play (there are three total Vulcan traits in the deck) and you may promote Sarek and optionally another Person from your Staging Area to Duty Officers, and he can support two extra Duty Officers with his passive ability. Now you can have Chekov and Sulu as Duty Officers, or any other combination that seems beneficial.
↑ back to top U.S.S. Enterprise-A (Ship) (Dev Cost 6 Dilithium or free if the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit) is logged)
The Enterprise-A is a beefed up version of the original. Two wild skills instead of one, with a Play action that allows you to warp or beam a card, then optionally discard a card to beam another card. You could easily beam three cards here in one turn, or four with Scotty as Duty Officer. This is incredibly useful to thin the deck and help accomplish missions. It has the Standard Activations for warp, beam, and promote, and scores four points.
↑ back to top U.S.S. Excelsior (Ship) (Dev Cost 6 Dilithium or free if Hikaru Sulu is your Duty Officer)
Sulu's ship deserves a discount if he's on Duty, right? Primarily seen in Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country, Sulu's Excelsior is the first to notice the environmental damage on the Klingon Homeworld. It was also sabotaged by Scotty in The Search for Spock, allowing for the theft of the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit).
The Excelsior gets two Wild skills with the Attack and Starfleet traits. When you use the Deploy Action, if your opponent has one or more ships at a neutral location, they take an Incident, and you can Find the Set a Course Directive. This makes the Excelsior a great choice for deploying multiple times, if you can get it out early enough. To that end, one of its Activations lets you spend a Dilithium to recall the Excelsior, which means you could theoretically give your opponent three Incidents in a turn. So, if you're in a multiplayer game with Kirk, watch out, and keep some Tellarites handy! There's also the standard warp and beam Activations, and it scores for Military Focus.
↑ back to top Vulcan (Location) (Dev Cost 4 Dilithium)
Vulcan has two Research skills and the Vulcan trait, and serves as Kirk and crew's asylum from the Federation following their theft and subsequent destruction of the Enterprise. Your Research must be four or higher to control, but once you do you may Find a Vulcan card (likely Captain Spock or Sarek, unless you found some market Vulcans), draw Utilize from your discard pile, and free play an Incident. If you don't have all of those things ready to go (because Spock is logged, you haven't developed Sarek, don't have Incidents in hand etc.) it's worth waiting to control Vulcan. But make sure you get it out before the end of the game. It scores for Influence focus in addition to an endgame operation that scores for your highest track multiplier (up to 4 in Advanced, 5 in Basic).
↑ back to top Time Warp (Directive) (Dev Cost 2 Dilithium and take an Incident)
Time travel. What, like it's hard? Just slingshot around the sun and have a Spock (or Borg Queen) with you.
Anyway, this custom directive gets the Time Travel and Anomaly traits. Its two play actions are free, allowing you to find any card, or alternatively recall a non-Time Travel card from your staging area. It scores for Research Focus. Keep in mind that Find also applies to Reserve unless indicated, so if you can use an Ancient Relay Station to get an early Development, you can really cut down on your time in your Reserves.
↑ back to top What Have I Done (Directive) (Dev Cost 3 Latinum)
Kirk's lament as he sets the Enterprise to self-destruct is a costly one, especially considering that we're in a post-scarcity world. But after paying that Latinum cost, you get a custom directive with the Attack trait. You can Log a deployed ship to send up to 3 away teams to the same location, and if it is secured you control it, and your opponent takes an Incident and dismisses one of their Duty Officers. Then this directive self-logs, scoring four points at game-end. If you used Needs of the Many on Spock, this is a perfect Directive to use on the Enterprise (Refit) . Then you can fish the Enterprise-A out of your Developments for free.
↑ back to top Kirk's Development Strategy
As always, development strategy is highly dependent on your game state, current tempo, and how close you are to the end of the game. Let's start with an early Dev, from Ancient Relay Station or completing The Undiscovered Country mission. If you have the ability to grab Time Warp early, I think you should do so. Its utility is just too good to pass up, both to Find cards and recall non-Time Travel cards. What wouldn't I grab first? Vulcan, probably. While the points are worth grabbing it at some point, there's not a ton of early necessity there. Same goes for the Excelsior or Enterprise-A , since you can enlist them both for free by having other conditions met. If you have a surplus of Dilithium, I'd consider the Excelsior, but it's fine to be patient and wait to get Sulu on Duty.
Speaking of Sulu, there are only two targets for logging a card using Needs of the Many. Frankly, Sulu is the better choice unless you intend to promote and combo off two Duty Officers, in which case I'd go with Sarek. Sulu's a good choice for an early Development using Needs of the Many, because then you can develop the Excelsior for free as some insurance against an aggressive opponent.
What Have I Done is a good card to get earlier on as well. If you log the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit) using this card, then you're set up for a free Development with the Enterprise-A . And with the four wild skills from the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit) and Excelsior , you can really start moving up tracks at that point. This will inform your market strategy, as you will almost always benefit from focus scoring because you have a built-in engine for maxing out tracks (certainly to the highest multiplier).
Kirk has the problem of "too many good things," so log some of those good things to make way for the best things that will stay in play and move your game forward. Remember, none of Kirk's Developments allow you to grab market cards, so don't be in such a rush to get them that you log all your directives that would allow you to grab more things from the market. And throughout all of this time, don't forget to use Kirk's Activation to rescue your directives from the discard pile!

Welcome to a purely hypothetical discussion of James T. Kirk and the common market. As always, I appreciate everyone sharing any really interesting combos you've found in the market in the comments on BGG or the Discord (in the Kirk conversation). Cards found in both the core box and To Boldly Go are discussed in the section for To Boldly Go. A quick note for Kirk: Because he has so many skills accessible to him, and incentives to move up tracks, pretty much all focus-scoring cards are valuable for him. I won't repeat that in the guide, but wanted to be clear that I recognized that even if some cards that have focus scoring are otherwise neutral for Kirk in terms of how much he can benefit from the Action/Table Operations.
↑ back to top Person Deck
To Boldly Go : Admiral Jarok is good for a Development but Kirk has enough attacks that I would avoid promoting him. Ambassador Gral is a great choice simply for Incident management and a "controlled" Burn win. Ash Tyler triggers Kirk's Reaction and you might be able to use him for hand size, but you won't have a situation with more Klingons than Starfleet. Hoshi Sato can be really helpful in searching for Vulcans to help complete missions, enough that I'd make her a priority. Jackabog can work well since Kirk has access to so many ships. Landru is fine but you'll have to give up some Person cards. Malik's Development gain should be pretty easy to get, and it's worth it to take him from your opponent. Sarina Douglas is a nice one to grab for even more wild skills, and you'll usually have the Incidents to fuel her and Doctor McCoy to benefit from her Play Action. Travis Mayweather is pretty good if you're not interested in exhausting Kirk for his table operations. For the remainder of the Person cards, Kirk doesn't have any unique advantages or disadvantages that I can see, as compared to other captains.
Second Contact: All five person cards from Second Contact are great for Kirk…but I don't particularly see them being bad for anybody. Both Ma'ah and D'Erika Tendi are great for ships, which makes Kirk even more deadly in the Neutral Zone. Parmen, K'Ranch, and Jennifer all benefit to different degrees as you gain person cards, which Uhura will help you with too. There's no bad persons, only bad circumstances.
Core Set: Ambassador Kamrag lets Kirk scan for some more Klingons, which is helpful as a potential completion route for The Undiscovered Country along with the Captain Reaction benefit. Kirk should have a decent number of Person cards to benefit from Joret Dal. Captain Dorg feels like he should be helpful, but without Weapon cards, he loses some of his bite. B-4 can help you there with getting Cargo, but is otherwise just of general benefit to all Captains. You'll probably meet Moriarty's Research requirement pretty early on, so he can be of benefit (especially in PvP games). I always like Laas and Peanut Hamper generally to fuel your engine. Admiral Pressman goes right along with Kirk's Attack cards, but the unique help ends there. Lwaxana Troi will help you benefit from Kirk multiple times and generally works well with his kit. Everyone else can be situationally useful but would likely need more cards than just Kirk's deck to combo well. Saurian Brandy is a pretty useful way to gain some Latinum, and Kirk should be able to meet that restriction relatively quickly. I really like Universal Translator in coordination with Uhura, if you're willing to take the time to go after that Encounter. It might not be worth it depending on how long it takes to get the Alien trait cards.
↑ back to top Cargo Deck
To Boldly Go: Augmentation Plague has some synergy with its Reaction, allowing Kirk to gain a Glory when he's putting Klingons in play, in addition to the Research Focus. Kirk has plenty of Ships that can benefit from Cloaking Device. EVA Suits may not be as useful because you already have Levitation Boots, but can still have some use. I like using Forced Singularity to find Scotty, which is more useful for Kirk than decks with no Engineer traits. Jago's Epigenetic Implant is fine for Kirk, but really only for the play action, because you're not going to have that many Augment cards, usually. Lirpa is pretty good for Kirk since he has a few Vulcans, only Soval finds it more useful. Medical Diagnostic Hat has the potential to result in a high card draw between Bones and Uhura's search for Alien trait cards. Everything else is pretty much neutral or situational for Kirk.
Second Contact: I would really be careful with Moopsy. Kirk is already going to be logging cards with Spock, so logging even more Person cards is a dangerous game. But card draw is great, and you can still benefit from your opponent logging Person cards. Protocol 12 works with Bones but you'll need to find some more Doctors. Gift Box has general utility but as I've noted before, Kirk really isn't too heavy a Cargo captain.
Core Set: Tribbles and Tachyon Detection Grid continue to be useful against specific captains, but aren't uniquely good for Kirk in my opinion. Biobed is great for Kirk and can allow you to Find Bones, or simply keep your Incidents managed. Mek'leth is most useful for Kirk for the Klingon trait. Kirk should be able to meet the restriction for Unstable Wormhole easily, and it's mostly just for him to help predict Encounters. Ferengi Wine has no real specific benefit I can see for Kirk.
↑ back to top Ship Deck
To Boldly Go: D'Kora Marauder will really only benefit Kirk if he can get a Latinum engine going, and his kit isn't really designed for that. Medusan Vessel is nice for gaining some more Telepaths but I'm not sure Kirk benefits from it uniquely except for the Alien trait. D'Var has the free play deploy, but beyond that its pretty standard for Kirk. Vor'Cha Attack Cruiser is great, because Kirk always wants Klingons (not thematically, of course). I really like Xindi-Aquatic Cruiser for the 2 Influence traits and free playing cards when warped, but Kirk doesn't benefit in a unique way.
The repeated Ships from the Core Box are all pretty much great for everyone. Borg Probe is always a great beaming Ship and Kirk wants it just as much as everyone else. D'Kyr can work in coordination with Kirk's other Vulcan cards and some market cards. Holographic Drone Ship should be playable with a Telepath (Spock or Sarek), though Kirk can probably also hit the seven Research requirement of the first free play action at some point. Enterprise-C is pretty great for Kirk, no real downsides. The Pasteur is probably best when looking for specific traits but Kirk benefits the same as everybody else. Xindi-Reptilians are good for getting some weapons and likely the scoring military focus, which Kirk should be able to advance on. Kazon is only good if you can grow an independent Latinum engine to fuel the Reaction. The Pakleds are actually pretty helpful for Kirk, simply because Cargo doesn't really come into his deck super easily.
Second Contact: The R.I.S. Talvath is pretty good because there's going to be plenty of Anomaly/Communication/Time Travel cards in play. Fesarius is good for the free play and free warp operations, and Kirk has plenty of Person cards that can be sent here (Spock could get logged twice).
Core Set: The Reliant is solid for Kirk based on his ease of moving up the Influence track. Same with the Son'a Battlecruiser and their six Military requirements. K'T'Inga Battlecruiser is a great option for Kirk because of the Klingon trait, Military Skill, and Away Team ability. Bird of Prey is good too, but I'm not sure Kirk gets any unique benefit. Voth Research Vessel is great for Kirk and you should be able to get the Encounter.
↑ back to top Ally Deck
To Boldly Go: The Kaelon II Science Ministry can be useful if you're willing to log Person/Directive cards, and Kirk's as willing if not more than most Captains. Orion Syndicate is great for Kirk due to his massive fleet of deck ships, and it'll fuel your Latinum production which you need pretty badly. I like Red Squadron for Kirk about as much as everyone else, and the more I play the more I enjoy the ability to beam as many cards as possible. Same with Salt Vampires, which in addition to the card draw are really thematic for Kirk. Tellarites are generally unnecessary with Bones but can help against an Incident-aggressive opponent. Unimatrix Zero and Vadic's Splinter Group are good but not specifically for Kirk over any other Captain. Vidiians can be really good for Kirk, merely because he has so many Person cards. But you do have a lot of ways to log them so be careful.
Arin'sen has the potential for a big play if you've been gathering Klingons for Kirk. Otherwise, Denobulans, Bolians, Bynars, Organians, and Kelpians don't really have any unique benefit for Kirk. Lethians can work if you need to draw Levitation Boots from your Discard pile, but since they log themselves that will only work one time. Suliban have that fun Time-Travel recall ability, and the alien trait can be useful.
Second Contact: Betazed Intelligence helps you find Sarek, but other than that you're probably only getting one turn with a slightly increased handsize before this logs. Illyrians are good because you can use Sarek to have 3 Duty officers, but you'll only get one shot at it unless you can leverage an Augment somehow. The play actions aren't bad for Kirk either. Nova Fleet is also great, though not any more for Kirk than for any other Captain.
Core Set: Halkan Council is good earlier on, before Kirk gets all his Attack cards, plus it's thematic. Tholians is good for pretty much anybody, but Kirk will be trying to play Strange New Worlds as often as possible so timing will matter and you probably won't have more than two or three controlled locations at most. Edosians is really good since Kirk needs those Encounters, and the restrictions should be achievable. Emerald Chain isn't anything special for Kirk beyond the Military gain. Karemma is always good for an extra action, but you may end up using them more for Latinum generation.
↑ back to top Encounters
To Boldly Go: Delphic Expanse Sphere is good for sending away teams to controlled locations to set up Strange New Worlds. Gomtuu is amazing for Kirk because it counts as an Encounter on a Ship by itself, meaning you only need to beam one more Encounter here to complete Kirk's mission. New Federation Applicants is fine for Incident management and card draw, and the wild focus offers some good flexibility for scoring. Species 10-C isn't amazing since logging planets is more beneficial from Strange New Worlds typically. Stone of Gol offers another Vulcan trait, and with a couple Telepaths in the deck you should be able to perform the
second play action. V'Ger and Koala have no downsides for Kirk, nor does the Guardian of Forever.
Core Set: Omega Particle for resource generation is both good for Kirk and provides a reliable focus scoring if you can max it out at four or five points. Boltzmann Brain is great for everybody, unless you just hit a point where you stopped taking Incidents. Sha Ka Ree is pretty good due to the Vulcan trait, and Kirk can afford to lose the ship needed to beam. Ancient Gene Fragments can be good if you get it early enough to set up Away Teams on locations before using Strange New Worlds to log them all, though I'm not sure how likely that is since you need to be logging those locations to get more Encounters. Iconian Gateway as a first Encounter is great for Kirk so you can start taking more locations, to log them, and get even more Encounters.
↑ back to top Promos
Sehlat is slightly better for Kirk than most considering Spock and Sarek in his deck. Enterprise B is both thematically cool for Kirk, but also a great way to use your Person cards for good. Wesley Crusher is always great, more so for Person-heavy captains like Kirk (just like Soval before him). Golden Statue of O'Brien can be triggered with Scotty, but otherwise you'll need the market to really utilize this card. Drones of Cube 90182, Frank Hollander, and the U.S.S. Cabot are all super situational and don't help Kirk in any specific way.
↑ back to top Individual Captain Considerations
Here's some thoughts on how Kirk stacks up against each captain so far, and some general principles I'd apply when facing them:
↑ back to top To Boldly Go
Georgiou: You can absolutely compete with Georgiou in the Neutral Zone, and you should do so in order to get locations for Strange New Worlds. You're also going to be competing for Klingon cards, so getting those whenever you can is a must. You're playing a pretty quick game, so try not to waste too much time and get into your Developments as soon as you can so that you can benefit from your slightly stronger kit.
Soval: Absolutely dominate Soval in the Neutral Zone, and don't be afraid to hit Strange New Worlds every turn as you control locations. His Developments are nowhere near as strong as yours, so beat him in points by overwhelming him with Encounters. Just be aware that you can exhaust the Encounter deck as he will also be able to get quite a few with IDIC.
Archer: Early game, Archer is fairly weak in the Neutral Zone due to his limited number of Ships and Away Teams. He does have some tricks up his sleeve when it comes to developments, but if you can keep him from resource-producing market cards, you can slow his game down a bit. A few choice attacks with the Excelsior should mess with his game.
Rebner: You can go toe-to-toe in the Neutral Zone when it comes to Ships, so feel free to fight and slow him down when you can, just be aware of his Developments. If you can get some defense from attacks, that would be optimal, and get those Military skill and focus cards. If the game appears to be rushing, then focus on your best tracks and Developments.
Khan: Thematic gold as a match-up, Kirk can play a fun game against Khan (Bot or PVP). You can get overwhelmed with a Burn loss, but if Khan is overwhelming you, get to the Excelsior and force Incidents right back at him. Trying to grab Augments to prevent him from gaining his Developments is helpful, but don't neglect your own game trying to mess with Khan's game. Lone exception here would be junking or gaining any cards in the market that have traits he's still looking for, when practical.
↑ back to top Second Contact
Pike: Pike has a lot of potential issues in the Neutral Zone, so don't be afraid to clog up his locations he's attempting to secure. While he can gain a lot of Ships later on, early in the game you can gain more Ships more quickly, so use that to your advantage. He benefits from cards with Skills due to his Status card, so consider junking those in the market if you can't get to them yourself. And remember, while a Burn victory may not be the sweetest, you can strategically time it well enough against Pike if he's being too greedy with how many Incidents he takes early in the game.
Riker: Riker's toolkit is a pretty standard Starfleet deck, just with a couple weird cards, but he actually mirrors you pretty closely. The U.S.S. Zheng He has a similar Incident text to the Excelsior and Proximity Blast has some similarities to your What Have I Done Directive. But he won't be able to move up tracks very quickly, and will basically ignore Influence, so keep that in mind when considering which focus cards to compete for. I think you can put up a pretty great fight in the Neutral Zone, but he will fight back harder than some Captains with his Riker Maneuver.
Freeman : I think Kirk should probably try for a Glory rush against Freeman whenever possible. Her early kit takes some time to get up and running efficiently, and if Kirk can gain early encounters and take as much Glory off the Stardate as possible, I think she'll generally struggle to keep up. That being said, she has more flexibility with all those Lower Deckers, so watch out for heavy-combo turns and be aware of what is most helpful for Freeman from the market.
↑ back to top Core Box
Picard: Kirk versus Picard is a level of iconic reserved for a Weird Al parody, and either in bot form or playing against a human opponent, you'll have your work cut out for you. The natural advantage Picard gets in gaining Ally cards is hard to match without significant point gain from the market or Encounters. If you can secure and control Neutral Zone locations with higher point values than Picard, that can help. He can definitely compete in the Neutral Zone but you should be able to box him out of the most important locations. Watch out for game rush and maybe don't give him too many Incidents (Troi helps him too much to risk a Burn victory).
Shran: The best advice I have for undercutting Shran is to hit the Neutral Zone hard with a fleet of ships and waves of Away Teams before he can get through his Reserves and use Andorian Commandos. If there's a way to gain weapon cards from the market before he does, it's not a bad idea. I think if Kirk's going to try for a rush-victory, Shran is one of the Captains worth rushing as you'll gain more from a shorter game than he will. Force him to expend a lot of resources in the Neutral Zone and take resource generating cards from the market before he can gain Dilithium (which will eventually turn into points via his endgame). .
Koloth: Advice against Koloth is always going to be the same: He can rush endgame quicker than anyone else, so your move will have to be making the most of the turns you have. Grab Ships early and often to fight him in the Neutral Zone. Don't be afraid to log a lot of cards with Spock to keep your deck thin and your game streamlined. Consider less ideal market cards with Glory on them to keep Koloth from his more lucrative Developments.
Sisko: I think Sisko's game is always going to be about location control, and he has the ships to back up that strategy. You can fight him there, but sometimes the best you can do is stalemate him in the Neutral Zone. That's not going to work for you without an alternative to the "Strange New Worlds for Encounter" game. Be willing to give him a Location or two, so long as you get one too. Then, beat him at the Encounter game and use those unique benefits to really knock Sisko down a peg.
Sela: I think Sela is a bit like Sisko for Kirk in the sense that she can really mess with his Neutral Zone game, which can mess with any leverage you have to take as many Encounters as possible quickly. Lock in and force her to take Incidents whenever you can. Try to rush the game before she can get to the Scimitar .
Burnham: Burnham is not particularly aggressive, but her game can go fairly quickly. As Kirk, you'll want to make sure she doesn't control a lot of locations while still keeping your Location control tempo. She can score really well between her Developments and Ally cards, so maybe don't give her a lot of opportunities to gain Ships from the junk. Don't give her the opportunity to take Encounters with Strange New Worlds, make her waste Dilithium via Stamets if she's going to insist on taking Encounters from you.
↑ back to top Putting It All Together
Kirk is going to be one of the most consistently victorious decks in the game, I'm predicting. The main reason for this should be relatively obvious: the Encounter reliance. Everything in his kit is telling you to get Encounters (for his Where No Man Has Gone Before mission) and to do that you'll need to use Strange New Worlds repeatedly, which can only be fueled by logging locations that you control. So that Neutral Zone supremacy is crucial to any Kirk game, and really that's best done with Ships and strategic logging timing of Starfleet Headquarters.
The other element to Kirk is his track movement. His multipliers all max out at 4x (10th space on each track), so it's not as if you absolutely need to hit a high threshold for those tracks. You do want to move up all three, but you have the necessary skills in your deck, easily. If you decide not to log the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit) , you'll have six wild skills among three ships and Starfleet Headquarters, Influence with Uhura and Sarek, Military with Sulu, two Research with Vulcan (not to mention the movement with Strange New Worlds) and one with Spock. That could mean nine total movement up Research, eight up Influence, and seven up Military just with three Utilize plays and no additional market cards. Is that going to happen every game? Probably not, but it's not impossible and is worth considering.
The deck wants to force you to balance those huge track-gain turns with the benefits you get from logging. Strange New Worlds gets you encounters, but logging Starfleet Headquarters will lose you that wild skill. You can log the U.S.S. Enterprise (Refit) to get the [i[Enterprise-A[/i] for free, but that's going to mean losing that extra wild skill. Honestly, you can play it differently than Kirk did, choosing to have a fleet of Enterprise ships at your disposal, just at a slightly higher cost.
There's also something else worth considering when it comes to piloting Kirk's deck, and that's how exactly you're supposed to pull off an Endgame Glory rush strategy. Spock, Uhura, and Chekov's Activations can all get you a Glory, but you'll probably be rushing your Developments more than anything else when playing as Kirk. That won't speed up the end of the game, so you have two options. First, find some cards in the market that contribute to gaining you Glory. Klingons often do, and since Kirk can use them to move up Military, that's a good option. The second strong option is to park plenty of Away Teams and ships on Locations you know your opponent will take in order to get the single Glory per token dismissed. That feels like a lot of work, but the goal isn't to necessarily be super efficient, but to get more Glory off the Stardate card.
Final thoughts: Kirk's deck is complex enough that I'm still discovering ways to go through the Developments efficiently depending on the game state, but not so complex that I can't give it to a newer player and let them try the game out with him. That's smart marketing as much as it is game-design. But beyond that, this is a Starfleet deck that says "you want to go big on Encounters? Let's do it." It gives you tools for reusing Directives repeatedly (primarily Kirk's Activation) and gives you freedom to trigger Strange New Worlds as often as is desired. All it asks of you is to keep controlling locations, and the Neutral Zone is usually big enough for the two of us. Throw that extra ship or Away Team on there and you'll be controlling planets every couple turns at minimum.
I hope you enjoyed this strategy guide for Captain Kirk's deck. Finally, we'll have to retire the "where is Kirk?" meme. Of course, I don't see Janeway yet, so I'm sure there will be plenty of requests for her next. But we do have Picard, Kirk, and Sisko now in the game. I wonder who we'll talk about next… Don't worry, I have a plan, y'all just have some faith. Of the heart.
